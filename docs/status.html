R2: the writing

After the page settles, the camera comes down to a reed pen, a qalam, and the seven āyāt are written, one chapter each. Scrolling chooses the āyah; the pen writes it at its own pace. The pen's path comes from the same data that reveals the ink, so the nib is always on the letter being formed. It lifts a little between strokes, more between words, and most at a line break, where the camera also eases back. The diacritics are flicked in after each word, as in v1.

The ink is alive. Under the nib is a wet, glossy bead. The stroke stays darker and glossier for a couple of seconds as it dries. Where the pen paused at the start and end of a stroke, the ink spreads a little into the grain. When an āyah is finished, the pen rises and the rosette after it opens petal by petal, with its numeral last.

The close shots needed sharper ink than v1 ever showed. A second, high-resolution ink layer now follows the camera, about six times sharper than the full page.

R1: the opening

The first chapter now plays by itself once the page has loaded. The stars come up out of the dark. A warm light comes up on the rolled parchment with a small flicker, like a candle catching, and then keeps breathing gently. Gold dust drifts in its beam. Meanwhile the camera pushes in slowly from far away to a low view of the roll's end, where its turns show.

Then scrolling unrolls the page. The camera travels with the roll as the paper pays out, then rises to look down on the opened sheet. The page chapter after it is still the R0 placeholder until R2 brings the writing.

R0: what is built

The dark world (v1's universe sky) and the parchment (v1's surface and material) on a phone, driven by the new engine. Three placeholder chapters exercise the rules: the opening rises out of the dark, scrolling unrolls the page, and the page settles flat. R1 to R4 replace them with designed shots.

The rules, and how they are kept

RuleHow v2 does it
Scroll picks the scene; time plays itScrolling asks for a chapter. The animation plays at its own pace. A fast flick plays up to 2.5× faster and never skips. Going back rewinds at 1.6×.
Nothing appears before it is readyEach chapter lists what it needs. Everything downloads from the first moment; each chapter's pieces are built and their shader programs prepared before it may start. If you arrive early, the page waits and says “The page is being prepared”.
One quality setting per devicePhone, laptop or desktop is chosen once when the page opens. It never changes while you watch. On phones the picture size ignores the browser bars sliding in and out.
Measured from day one?hud=1 shows the frame rate. ?trace=1 records every frame. A scripted phone session plays the piece and checks every frame.
The text never changesv1's text, ink and gold pipeline is carried over unchanged. Rebuilt here, it produces every file byte for byte, and every build checks the seven āyāt letter for letter.

Frames that stalled while the harness itself was taking a screenshot are listed separately in the report. They are not counted above.

New in R2: the browser keeps calling the frame loop 60 times a second even when the graphics chip falls behind, so frame intervals alone can look perfect while the picture lags. The sessions now also measure how long the chip takes to finish its work. The desktop profile runs closest to its limit (about 14 ms of a 16.7 ms frame at 2880 × 1800), which the R6 performance pass will look at. When the ink arrives late, its preparation waits for a quiet moment and costs two or three 33–50 ms frames while the picture is still.

Decisions

  • 2026-10-13Pace: v1's rhythm for letters, word gaps and diacritics is kept (at 70 seconds per unit of v1's scroll). Wherever the next stroke begins away from where the last one ended, the pen gets time to travel, at 60 cm/s. A line break gets at least 1.1 s. Āyāt 1–6 write in 2.8–5.4 s; āyah 7, ten words, in 10.8 s.
  • 2026-10-13The camera's look point follows the pen through a time filter: about one second of the pen's path, weighted slightly ahead. It eases back by up to 1.5× at each line break.
  • 2026-10-13Assets that arrive after the intro wait for a quiet moment before preparing: the playhead at rest, or the reader waiting for that very asset. On a slow connection the ink can then never cost a frame in the middle of the unroll.
  • 2026-10-13The dust moves along fixed drifting paths worked out directly on the graphics card. Nothing has to be simulated from frame to frame, so the compute pass the plan suggested isn't needed, and it runs the same on WebGPU and WebGL2. The motes live in the light's cone, so almost every one is in the beam. Phones get 35% of the desktop count.
  • 2026-10-13Close shots are authored on a 16:10 screen. A narrower screen stands back by the square root of the width difference (about 1.65× on a phone), so the same move still fits.
  • 2026-10-13Fixed the parchment's shading normals, which were flat per grid row (visible as bands up close). v1 has the same flaw but only shows the page from further away; v1 is left untouched.
  • 2026-10-12Profiles: phone at up to 1.75 device pixels per point, 1024 shadow map with a simple soft filter, no page inertia, particles at 35%. Laptop and desktop get v1's softer shadows.
  • 2026-10-12Pace limits: a flick plays up to 2.5× the authored speed, and going back plays at 1.6×.
  • 2026-10-12On a phone the reading view fills the screen with the text block. A wide screen keeps more of the page around it.
  • 2026-10-12The opening waits until the intro has fully faded, then the light comes up.
